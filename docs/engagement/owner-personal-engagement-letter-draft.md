This letter confirms the terms on which [Firm] (the "Firm") will represent you personally, alongside its separate engagement by [Entity] (the "Company"). Please read it, sign, and return it. It takes effect when signed.

## 1. Two clients, two engagements

The Firm represents the Company under a separate letter dated [ ]. In that engagement the Company is the client, and the Firm owes its duties to the Company. Under this letter the Firm represents you individually. These are two engagements with two clients, and the Firm will keep two files.

## 2. Scope of your personal engagement

[Select:]

**(A) Personal documents.** The Firm will prepare and maintain: a durable power of attorney with business powers coordinated to the Company's plan; [will / revocable trust] with provisions for your interest in the Company; a designation of health care surrogate and living will; and [other]. Fee: $[ ] flat, earned on delivery of signed documents. Maintenance of these documents is included in the Company's Phase 2 fee by allocation (Section 6), with no separate subscription charged to you.

**(B) Coordination only.** Your personal documents are prepared by [other counsel]. The Firm will not draft them. The Firm's work for you is limited to reviewing your personal documents for consistency with the Company's plan at onboarding and annual review, reporting inconsistencies to you and, with your consent, to your other counsel, and advising you on the items in Section 4. No separate fee; this work is allocated as in Section 6.

**(C) Your role after you step back** (by change order under this letter, approved in writing before work starts). The Firm will prepare, for you, the terms of your role after you step back: [consulting or employment agreement; redemption or buy-sell terms for your interest; non-compete; the step-down of your authority]. Where the Company is the other party to those terms, the Firm will act for both you and the Company only if the Firm reasonably believes it can represent each of you competently and diligently, and you and the Company each give informed consent, confirmed in writing in that change order, after the Firm explains the implications, advantages and risks of acting for both (Rule 4-1.7(b) and (c)). The Firm will not act for both, and one of you will need other counsel for those terms, if: the Company has another owner; the person acting for the Company on those terms is the buyer or will control the Company after you step back; a payment to you under those terms would be a distribution that section 605.0405(1), Florida Statutes, prohibits; or you and the Company disagree on the terms. Fee: $[ ], stated in the change order.

Anything else — Medicaid or long-term-care planning, asset-protection planning, estate or gift tax planning, guardianship, or representation in any dispute — is outside this engagement and will be proposed by change order under this letter if you want it.

## 3. Conflict disclosure and consent (Rule 4-1.7)

Your interests and the Company's are aligned on most of this work and may differ on some of it: who receives your interest and on what terms; whether the Company continues, is sold, or is wound down; valuation; how sale proceeds are paid and taxed; the treatment of guaranties; your role, pay and restrictions after you step back, and any redemption of your interest; [and, if there are other owners, buyout terms and price]. If the Firm concludes that a difference has become a conflict it cannot resolve consistently with its duties to both, it will tell you and the Company and may have to withdraw from one or both engagements. Information you give the Firm in this engagement may be used in the Company's engagement for the purposes of Section 4, and the Company has given the corresponding consent; otherwise each file is confidential to its client.

By signing, you confirm that the Firm has explained these risks, that you have had the opportunity to consult independent counsel, and that you consent to the Firm's representation of both you and the Company on these terms.

## 4. Coordination consent

You consent to the Firm:

(a) reviewing your personal documents, whoever drafted them, for consistency with the Company's plan at the coordination points listed in Schedule C;
(b) using what it learns from each engagement to keep the other consistent;
(c) reporting the *status* of each coordination point (in place and consistent: yes/no) in the Company's workspace, where any other authorized Company user may see it, but never the contents of your personal documents; and
(d) communicating with your other counsel, your CPA, and your financial advisor about coordination items, within the scope you approve on the weekly call.

You may withdraw consent (d) at any time in writing. Withdrawing consents (a)–(c) ends the coordination work and the Firm's ability to keep the two plans consistent; the Firm will tell the Company that coordination has ended, without saying why.

## 5. The workspace

You have access to a personal tier of the Company's workspace that holds your personal documents and the full coordination record. The Company's tier does not. The terms of the Company's letter on use of AI and the limits of the assistant (its Section 6) apply here. The disclosures in Section 6(c) of that letter (what is sent to the AI provider, the provider's terms, the retention the Firm cannot prevent, notice of breach or legal process, and notice of changes) apply equally to your personal information. By signing, you give your own consent on those terms. You may withdraw it in writing at any time without affecting the Company's consent, and the reverse is also true. Information from your personal tier is never sent to the provider with a request about the Company. Do not enter medical information in the workspace; raise health matters on the call. If a question about your own capacity ever arises, the Firm will address it with you directly and outside the workspace, under separate terms.

## 6. Fees and allocation

Fees under this letter are stated above and in any change order. Where the Company pays a fee that includes work for you (the Phase 2 allocation, or a change order resolving a coordination item), the Firm will state the allocation on the invoice, and you acknowledge that the Company's payment of a fee for your benefit is a matter between you and the Company [and its other owners, and your CPA, for tax treatment]. The Firm takes no position on the tax treatment of allocated fees. Nothing is collected in advance.

## 7. Documents and termination

Documents the Firm prepares for you are yours on delivery and remain valid regardless of the status of either engagement. Either of us may end this engagement on written notice; ending it does not end the Company's engagement, and ending the Company's engagement does not end this one, except that the coordination work in Section 2(B) and Section 4 depends on both.

## Drafter's notes

- § 2(C) and entity letter § 7 checked against Rule 4-1.7 text and comment (pasted by Josh, 2026-11-03; amended through June 1, 2014). Basis: the comment permits common representation in a negotiation "where the clients are generally aligned in interest even though there is some difference of interest among them," and bars it where interests are "fundamentally antagonistic"; the four carve-outs are the antagonistic cases. Each change order also needs the disinterested-lawyer check (comment, "Consultation and consent").
- Rule 4-1.13 (text pasted by Josh, 2026-11-03; amended through May 22, 2006): (e) lets the Company's consent come from "an appropriate official of the organization other than the individual who is to be represented, or by the shareholders"; the comment applies the rule to unincorporated associations, with members as the equivalent constituents. A sole member may therefore consent for the Company, signing in that capacity. Because the owner then consents on both sides, the disinterested-lawyer check (4-1.7 comment) and the solvency carve-out carry the protection. (d) requires explaining who the client is when interests are adverse; entity letter § 7 does that in writing.
- § 605.0405 confirmed 2026-11-03 (2025 Fla. Stat., flsenate.gov; history s. 2, ch. 2013-180). A redemption is a distribution "by purchase, redemption, or other acquisition of a transferable interest" ((3)(a)); it is barred if afterward the Company could not pay its debts as they become due in the ordinary course ((1)(a)) or its assets would be less than its liabilities plus senior dissolution preferences ((1)(b)). The § 2(C) carve-out now cites (1) directly, covering both tests.
- For R2 drafting (not the letter): an installment redemption is measured at the earlier of the Company's transfer or incurring the debt, or the owner ceasing to own the interest ((3)(a)); the note is at parity with general unsecured creditors unless subordinated ((4)); a note payable only when a distribution could then be made is not a liability for the test, and each payment is tested when made ((5)).
- § 605.0102(17) confirmed 2026-11-03: "distribution" includes a redemption and "a transfer to a member in return for the member's relinquishment of any right to participate as a member in the management or conduct of the company's activities and affairs"; it excludes "reasonable compensation for present or past service" and payments "under a bona fide retirement plan or other bona fide benefits program." For R2: consulting pay above reasonable compensation, and any payment for stepping down from management, are distributions tested under § 605.0405.
- § 605.0406 confirmed 2026-11-03: a consenting member or manager who fails § 605.04091 is personally liable to the Company for the excess (1); a person who receives a distribution knowing it violated § 605.0405 is personally liable for the excess (3); actions are barred 2 years after the distribution (5). In a single-member company the owner consents and receives, so both apply to the owner.
- § 605.04091 confirmed 2026-11-03: duties of loyalty and care run to the Company and its members (1). Loyalty includes "refraining from dealing with the company … as, or on behalf of, a person having an interest adverse to the company, except to the extent that a transaction satisfies the requirements of s. 605.04092" ((2)(b)). Care is breached only by gross negligence, recklessness, willful misconduct, or a knowing violation of law (3). Reliance on counsel's or an accountant's statements is protected (6) unless the person knows it is unwarranted (7). For R2: the owner, as member or manager, deals with the Company on an adverse interest in setting their own consulting pay and redemption terms, so R2 terms must satisfy § 605.04092. Consenting to a redemption that fails § 605.0405 without reasonable reliance is the § 605.0406(1) trigger.
- Walk to before first execution: § 605.04092 (conflict-of-interest transactions).
