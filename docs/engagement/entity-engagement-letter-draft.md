Re: Engagement for Continuity Planning and Ongoing Counsel — Subscription

Dear [Owner]:

This letter confirms that [Entity] (the "Company") has engaged [Firm] (the "Firm") on the terms below. Please read it, sign where indicated, and return it. The engagement begins when we receive the signed letter and the onboarding fee.

## 1. The client

The Company is the client. The Firm represents the Company as an organization, acting through its authorized officers (Rule 4-1.13). The Firm does not represent you individually, any other owner, any employee, or any family member in this engagement. If you want the Firm to prepare your personal documents (power of attorney, will, trust, health directive), that is a separate engagement under a separate letter, with the written consent Rule 4-1.7 requires, and the Firm will raise the question at onboarding.

**Coordination with the owner's personal plan.** The Company's exit plan and [Owner]'s personal estate plan depend on each other at defined points (who receives the ownership interest, who holds authority on day one, how a buyout is funded, how sale proceeds are treated, and who speaks for the family, among others). The Firm will check these points for consistency at onboarding and at each annual review, whether [Owner]'s personal documents are prepared by the Firm or by other counsel. The Company consents to the Firm using what it learns in the Company's engagement to keep [Owner]'s personal plan consistent with it, and acknowledges that [Owner] gives the corresponding consent in the personal engagement letter. The Company's workspace will show whether each coordination point is in place and consistent; it will not display the contents of [Owner]'s personal documents.

## 2. Scope and project end

The engagement is a single project: preparing the Company for the exit of [Owner] from the business, on the date the Company sets or on any earlier date an event makes necessary. The project ends at the Company's exit event (a sale, transfer, retirement, or orderly wind-down), when it occurs, followed by the post-exit items listed in the plan. Any target exit date on the project schedule is a milestone set by the Company and may be moved by the Company at any time; it is not a commitment by either party.

**Phase 1 — Onboarding (approximately six weeks).** The Firm will prepare the Company's continuity plan: the trigger definition, interim-manager authority (operating-agreement amendment or resolution), cash rule, instruction letter, guaranty and lease schedule, default-branch designation, and a stakeholder continuity statement. The plan is complete when these are signed and delivered. The Firm will also deliver a written list of open items the onboarding identified but the plan does not address, each with a price.

**Phase 2 — Project management to exit (month to month, through the exit event).** The Firm provides:

(a) a weekly scheduled call of up to [30/60] minutes with [attorney];
(b) maintenance of the plan: the state file, the contingency-status review, the coordination check described in Section 1, the annual review on the Company's anchor date, and reissue of the continuity statement;
(c) in-house counsel work of the kinds listed in Schedule A ("Included Work"); and
(d) access to the Firm's client workspace described in Section 6.

**Change orders.** Work of the kinds listed in Schedule B ("Quoted Work") is outside the subscription and is added to the project by change order: a written scope and fee the Firm proposes, ordinarily on the weekly call, and the Company approves in writing before work starts. The project budget in the workspace lists approved change orders, work completed, and estimated allowances for items not yet scoped. Allowances are estimates for planning only; nothing is payable on an allowance until it becomes an approved change order and the work is done. Litigation, regulatory proceedings, and matters with opposing counsel are always Quoted Work. Schedule A controls over any workspace classification.

## 3. Fees and payment

**Onboarding fee:** $[ ], payable at signing. This is a flat fee for Phase 1, earned on delivery of the signed plan. [If nonrefundable: *This fee is nonrefundable once the plan is delivered. If the engagement ends before delivery, the Company will receive a refund of the unearned portion, determined by the work completed.* — Rule 4-1.5(e) written statement.]

**Subscription fee:** $[ ] per month, billed on the first of each month in arrears for the preceding month's availability and services, due within 15 days. The subscription fee is earned when billed and is not an advance fee; it is not deposited in trust.

**Change orders:** as approved in writing, billed on completion or monthly, as the change order states. No fee is collected in advance for a change order or an allowance.

**Costs:** filing fees and third-party costs are billed at cost. The Firm's use of AI and software tools is part of its overhead and is not billed to the Company.

The Company may end Phase 2 at any time on 30 days' written notice, whether or not the exit event has occurred. Fees for the notice month remain payable. Phase 2 otherwise ends with the post-exit items after the exit event.

## 4. What the Company keeps

Every document the Firm prepares for the Company belongs to the Company on delivery and remains valid whether or not the subscription continues. Ending the subscription does not revoke, suspend, or condition any document. What ends is the Firm's maintenance of the plan, the weekly call, Included Work, and workspace access; the continuity statement will not be reissued after its current expiry.

## 5. The weekly call and decisions

Decisions are made by the Company on the weekly call or in writing. The Firm will not take any step with a third party, incur a cost, or begin Quoted Work without the Company's approval. Each call ends with a written summary of decisions and next steps, which the Firm will treat as the record unless the Company objects within [5] business days.

## 6. The client workspace and use of AI

The Firm provides a secure online workspace for the Company's engagement. It holds the Company's plan documents, the state file, the project board, and an assistant that answers from those documents.

(a) **What it does.** The assistant reports what the Company's documents say, collects facts the Firm needs, and places matters on the weekly agenda. It also accepts material the Company obtained from other sources, including public AI tools, and compares it to the Company's documents.

(b) **What it does not do.** The assistant does not give legal advice, evaluate whether any outside material is correct, make decisions, or quote fees. Nothing the assistant produces is advice of the Firm until an attorney has reviewed and released it. Only the attorney's statements on the call or in a released document are the Firm's advice.

(c) **Confidentiality, AI providers, and consent.**

(1) *What is sent.* To produce the assistant's responses, the Firm's server sends the Company's question and the Company's workspace documents that bear on it to an AI model provider, currently Anthropic, PBC, through the provider's commercial application programming interface. The Firm does not use consumer AI products for the Company's matter.

(2) *Provider terms.* The Firm uses the provider under commercial terms that prohibit use of the Company's information to train models and that require confidentiality. [The Firm's account operates under a zero-data-retention arrangement: the provider does not store the Company's questions, documents, or the responses after each response is returned.]

(3) *Retention the Firm cannot prevent.* Even under that arrangement, the provider may retain a request and its response where the law requires it, and where the provider's automated safety systems flag the request, in which case the provider may keep it for up to two years. The Firm cannot control or guarantee against either.

(4) *What the Firm does not send.* The Firm does not send any individual's health information. It does not send information from [Owner]'s personal tier with a request about the Company, or the Company's information with a request about [Owner]'s personal matters. It does not use the provider's file-storage, batch-processing, or hosted-agent services, which keep data after a response.

(5) *Changes.* Before the Firm sends the Company's information to a different provider, or under materially different retention terms, it will tell the Company in writing.

(6) *Consent.* The Company consents to the Firm's use of the provider on these terms for its matter, and to the attorney's visibility into everything entered in the workspace. The Company may withdraw this consent in writing at any time. The assistant then stops for the Company's matter, and the Firm continues the engagement by call and correspondence [at the same subscription fee].

(7) *Health information.* The Company agrees not to enter medical information about any individual in the workspace. If health is relevant to a matter, the Company will raise it on the call, and the Firm will handle it outside the workspace.

(d) **Public AI tools.** Communications between the Company and a public AI tool are not communications with the Firm and may not be privileged. The Firm recommends that the Company bring questions to the workspace or the call rather than to public tools, and the workspace is designed to accept whatever the Company has already obtained elsewhere.

## 7. Conflicts and other owners

[If multi-member:] The Firm represents the Company, not its members. The Firm has disclosed that it is not representing [other members], and that the interests of members may diverge from the Company's, including on succession, buyout, and valuation. Each member has been advised that they may retain separate counsel.

## 8. Capacity of the signer

The Firm's engagement assumes the Company's authorized signer has capacity. If a question of capacity arises, the Firm will address it on the call; matters involving a signer's capacity are handled outside the workspace under separate terms.

## 9. Termination and file

Either party may end the engagement on written notice, subject to Section 3. On termination the Firm will return the Company's file and documents within [10] business days and will keep its own copy as the Rules require.

## Schedule A — Included Work (subscription)

- Reviewing a contract, lease, or proposal the Company receives and advising on the call
- Answering legal questions about the Company's operations, employees, customers, vendors, or regulators
- Reviewing a demand, notice, or inquiry received by the Company and advising on response
- Reviewing a lender's, customer's, or regulator's continuity or key-person questionnaire and preparing the Company's responses from the plan
- Annual minutes, consents, and routine Sunbiz filings
- Maintaining the plan, state file, and continuity statement

## Schedule B — Quoted Work (approved in writing before starting)

- Drafting or negotiating a contract, lease, amendment, or settlement
- Responding to a demand, notice, or inquiry on the Company's behalf
- Any matter with a court, arbitrator, agency, or opposing counsel
- Entity formation, restructuring, merger, sale, or dissolution
- Employment agreements, retention agreements, buy-sell agreements, and valuation work
- Drafting the continue, sell, or wind-down branch beyond the plan's default-branch designation
- Family governance when the exit is to a family member or insider: facilitated family meeting, family charter, next-generation readiness track
- Drafting to resolve a coordination item (e.g., amending the operating agreement to admit a trust, restructuring buy-sell funding), allocated between this engagement and the personal engagement as the change order states
- Any work for a person other than the Company
