# Rule 4-7.13, Rules Regulating The Florida Bar

Text as pasted by Josh on 2026-11-03 (amended through June 22, 2023, effective August 21, 2023, SC22-1294). Key passages relied on:

- (a): an advertisement is deceptive or inherently misleading if it "(1) contains a material statement that is factually or legally inaccurate; (2) omits information that is necessary to prevent the information supplied from being misleading; or (3) implies the existence of a material nonexistent fact."
- (b)(1) predictions or guaranties of results; (b)(2) unverifiable past results; (b)(3) unverifiable characterizations or comparisons; (b)(4) areas of practice not practiced; (b)(5) voices or images implying the speaker is the lawyer; (b)(9) testimonial conditions, including the disclaimer "that the prospective client may not obtain the same or similar results"; (b)(10) implied Bar approval.
- Comment, predictions: process statements such as "protect the client's rights, protect the client's assets, or protect the client's family" are permissible; aspirational words "goal," "strive," "dedicated," "mission," "philosophy"; modifiers "try, pursue, may, seek, might, could, and designed to."
- Comment, comparisons: "the best," "second to none," "the finest" will generally violate the rule.
- Comment, past results: advertising them requires the client's informed consent under 4-1.6, even if public.
