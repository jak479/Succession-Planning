# Rule 4-3.7, Rules Regulating The Florida Bar

Text as pasted by Josh on 2026-11-03 (amended March 23, 2006, effective May 22, 2006, 933 So.2d 417). Key passages relied on:

- (a): "A lawyer shall not act as advocate at a trial in which the lawyer is likely to be a necessary witness on behalf of the client unless: (1) the testimony relates to an uncontested issue; (2) the testimony will relate solely to a matter of formality …; (3) the testimony relates to the nature and value of legal services rendered in the case; or (4) disqualification of the lawyer would work substantial hardship on the client."
- (b): another lawyer in the firm may act as advocate unless precluded by rule 4-1.7 or 4-1.9.
- Comment: on (a)(4), "It is relevant that one or both parties could reasonably foresee that the lawyer would probably be a witness." A substantial conflict between the client's and the lawyer's testimony is a 4-1.7 conflict; the problem arises "whether the lawyer is called as a witness on behalf of the client or is called by the opposing party."
