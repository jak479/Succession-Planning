# Rule 4-1.8, Rules Regulating The Florida Bar

Text as pasted by Josh on 2026-11-03 (amended through November 9, 2017, effective February 1, 2018, 234 So. 3d 577). Key passages relied on:

- (a): business transactions with a client require fair terms disclosed in writing, written advice to seek independent counsel, and informed consent in a writing signed by the client. Comment: the rule "applies to lawyers engaged in the sale of goods or services related to the practice of law. See rule 4-5.7. It does not apply to ordinary fee arrangements between client and lawyer, which are governed by rule 4-1.5."
- (f): compensation from a third party only if "(1) the client gives informed consent; (2) there is no interference with the lawyer's independence of professional judgment or with the client-lawyer relationship; and (3) information relating to representation of a client is protected as required by rule 4-1.6." Comment: a conflict exists under 4-1.7(a) "when the third-party payer is a co-client"; consent then confirmed in writing.
- (h): no prospective limit on malpractice liability unless permitted by law and the client is independently represented.
- Comment, fiduciary appointments: the lawyer "should advise the client in writing concerning who is eligible to serve as a fiduciary, that a person who serves as a fiduciary is entitled to compensation, and that the lawyer may be eligible to receive compensation for serving as a fiduciary in addition to any attorney's fees," with informed consent confirmed in writing.
- (k): prohibitions in (a)–(i) are imputed to all lawyers in the firm.
