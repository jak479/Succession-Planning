# Copy rules for marketing and client surfaces (Rules 4-7.13, 4-7.14)

Source: Rule 4-7.13 text pasted by Josh, 2026-11-03 (docs/sources/fl-rule-4-7-13.md). Rule 4-7.14 text pasted 2026-11-03 (docs/sources/fl-rule-4-7-14.md). Rule 4-7.11 text pasted 2026-11-03 (docs/sources/fl-rule-4-7-11.md): "advertising" means "all forms of communication seeking legal employment, both written and spoken," in any medium, including websites (a); it also covers "communications made to referral sources about legal services" (c). Scope for this product:
- **Covered:** the website and any marketing; the non-client front door; demos shown to prospects; anything sent to CPAs, wealth managers, bankers or other advisors who might refer owners (4-7.11(c)), including materials for the Phase 3 advisor team; any proposal to an existing client for new legal employment, which includes change-order offers on the board and the Phase 3 offer at the exit, unless an exemption applies.
- **Rule 4-7.20 read 2026-11-03** (docs/sources/fl-rule-4-7-20.md): it exempts communications only "from the filing requirements of rule 4-7.19," not from 4-7.13 or 4-7.14. So change-order offers and the Phase 3 offer to existing clients need no filing ((d): "a communication mailed only to existing clients, former clients, or other lawyers") but must still meet every content rule here; rule 12 stands. Also exempt from filing: the website ((g)) and communications a prospect requested, such as front-door replies ((e)).
- **Filing needed:** anything outside those exemptions, notably materials sent to CPAs, wealth managers or bankers as referral sources: (d) covers only clients, former clients and lawyers. Rule 4-7.16 (permissible content, which defines the (a) exemption) not yet read.

## Filing and review (Rule 4-7.19, read 2026-11-03; docs/sources/fl-rule-4-7-19.md)
- **Deadline:** file each non-exempt advertisement "at least 20 days prior to the lawyer's first dissemination," in the manner posted on the Bar's website ((a)). Build 20 days into every launch of referral-source materials.
- **Contents ((h)):** the ad in disseminated form; transcript and full text; sample envelope if mailed; every medium, frequency and period of use; the responsible lawyer (Josh); the fee set by the Bar's executive director (amount not stated in the rule; check the Bar's website); substantiation if requested.
- **Effect ((b), (f)):** no Bar communication within 15 days of a complete filing means no discipline for disseminating it; a notice of compliance binds the Bar in a grievance unless the ad contains a misrepresentation not apparent on its face. Review is facial only; accuracy of facts stays Josh's responsibility ((e)).
- **Refile ((i))** when a change makes the ad possibly false or misleading, e.g., a price change, a change to what Phase 3 includes, or a change in licensure.
- **Keep ((j))** a copy of each ad for 3 years after its last use, with when and where it was used; for identical mailings, one copy plus the recipient list.
- **Safe harbor for exempt items ((d), comment):** an exempt communication may be submitted for an advisory opinion, and a notice of compliance gives immunity from grievance liability except for misrepresentations not apparent on the face. A whole website cannot be submitted, but a specific page or statement can. **Recommended submissions before launch:** (1) the family-office clarifier sentence with the term in context; (2) the client home headline and the "Off your plate" lines; (3) the change-order offer template from the board (rule 12); (4) the front door's fixed client-facing forms and the "Automated assistant · not a lawyer" label.
- **New York:** Josh is also admitted in New York; materials disseminated there and not targeted at Florida residents follow New York's rules instead (comment, lawyers admitted in other jurisdictions).

1. **Process and aspiration, not results.** No statement a prospect "can reasonably interpret as a prediction or guaranty of success or specific results" ((b)(1)). Describe what the plan or the Firm does, or use the comment's modifiers: try, pursue, may, seek, might, could, designed to. The comment allows "protect the client's rights, protect the client's assets, or protect the client's family" and aspirational words: goal, strive, dedicated, mission, philosophy.
   - Applied 2026-11-03: "the plan is designed to keep the business running while you are out"; "the plan is designed to hold if you cannot come back"; Stage 4 "designed to be run by your office and your advisors."
   - The theme, "From being in business to live, to being in the business of living," is aspirational and promises no legal result.
2. **The family office is the client's.** Never imply the Firm provides investment management, tax preparation, accounting or bill pay ((a)(2) material omission; (a)(3) nonexistent fact; (b)(4) areas the Firm does not practice). Any marketing that uses "family office" carries: "Legal services only. Your family office is the advisor team we coordinate; investment, tax and accounting services come from your own advisors." Also required by the SEC family office rule reading (docs/engagement/family-office-engagement-outline.md). Under 4-7.14(a)(1)–(2), "family office" is open to a reasonable reading (a full-service office that manages money) that would be materially misleading; the sentence above is the clarifying information (b) permits, so it must sit with the term, not in a footer.
3. **No unverifiable characterization or comparison** ((b)(3)): no "best," "leading," "premier," "trusted by." "The quarterback for owners whose wealth is the business" describes the Firm's role, not its skill or record; keep it a role statement and never add a comparative.
4. **Past results** only if objectively verifiable, with the client's informed consent under 4-1.6 even if public ((b)(2), comment).
5. **Testimonials** only on what clients can judge (courtesy, promptness, efficiency, demeanor), actual experience, not drafted by the Firm, nothing of value given, with the disclaimer that a prospective client may not obtain the same or similar results ((b)(9)).
6. **Experience claims** count only the lawyer's own time as a lawyer (comment, material omissions). Licensure: "licensed in Florida and New York" is a statement of fact; no implication of Bar approval ((b)(10)).
7. **AI front door.** The label "Automated assistant · not a lawyer" stays on every assistant message; it also prevents the (b)(5) impression that the speaker is the lawyer.
8. **Workspace product.** Do not describe the workspace or the AI as doing legal work it does not do; it reports documents, asks for facts, schedules, or defers (CLAUDE.md rule 1).
9. **Specialist, expert, certified** (4-7.14(a)(4)–(6)). Do not call Josh or the Firm a "specialist" or "expert" in exit, succession or family-office planning, or "certified," unless certified under the Florida Certification Plan (or an accredited plan) in that area, or the claim is objectively verifiable from education, training, experience or substantial involvement. Permitted without either: "concentrates in," "focuses on," "limits the practice to," if true (comment).
10. **Fees in advertising** (4-7.14(a)(7)). Any ad that states the onboarding fee or subscription price must also disclose every other fee or expense the client might owe: change orders (Schedule B), the owner's personal engagement fees, the Phase 3 engagement, and any filing or third-party costs. An advertised fee or range must be honored for 90 days unless the ad states a shorter period. Change-order "estimates" in an ad are advertised ranges and carry the same 90-day duty.
11. **Awards and ratings** (4-7.14(a)(3), comment): only bona fide ones, by actual name and date.
12. **Change-order offers on the board** (rule 10 applied; 4-7.20 exempts them from filing only): each estimate shown to a client states everything the client may owe for that work (fee range plus any filing or third-party costs) and carries an "estimate good through" date at least 90 days out.
